\begin{abstract}

Fine-tuning can reshape model behavior beyond the source task, but whether structured decision training transfers reusable operations and supports reliable rule following remains unclear. Using Dou Dizhu, a rule-rich card game, we compare three open-weight language models before adaptation, after ordinary domain adaptation, and after rule-constrained adaptation. On a 300-item benchmark we construct to evaluate source-domain decision operations in different contexts, ordinary adaptation produces strongly model- and category-dependent transfer. Rule-constrained adaptation achieves higher overall accuracy than both baselines for all three models, although category-level effects remain selective. Paired decision probes measure how introducing an explicit prohibition changes the log-probability margin between the strongest legal alternative and the prohibited action. For Qwen2.5-7B, rule-constrained adaptation substantially increases this relative preference shift, yet greedy decoding selects the prohibited action in all 60 probes. Exhaustive single-head ablation on the same model reveals heterogeneous changes in causal dependence concentrated in a small subset of attention heads. Joint ablation of heads with the largest increases in causal contribution reduces the measured preference shift more than global-random and layer-bin-matched random controls. Together, these findings demonstrate selective task transfer and concentrated changes in the causal support for constraint-induced preference shifts. Gains in task accuracy and stronger relative preference shifts can coexist with persistent rule violations during generation.

\end{abstract}
